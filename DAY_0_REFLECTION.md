# Day 0 Reflection

## 1. Why do I want to study business beyond my current analytics role?

My current role has given me strong technical depth — I can build models, run analyses, and generate insights — but I often sit downstream of the actual business decisions those insights feed into. I want to understand the *why* behind business models, competitive strategy, and executive decision-making, not just the *what* of the numbers. Studying business will help me connect analytics to the core levers of the business — revenue, cost, growth, and customer value — so that instead of just reporting on outcomes, I can shape the decisions that drive them. Ultimately, I want analytics to become a strategic input to the business rather than a reporting function sitting alongside it.

## 2. What type of business leader do I aspire to become in the next five years?

I aspire to become a CEO- or COO-type leader — someone who is analytically strong but also deeply grounded in operations and strategy. I want to be the kind of leader who builds sustainable, profitable, and scalable businesses, using data not as an afterthought but as a core part of how decisions get made. This means combining the rigor of an analytical mindset with the judgment and vision needed to set direction, allocate resources, and build durable competitive advantage.

## 3. Which three companies do I most admire, and why?

**Google, Walmart, and Meta.**

I admire these companies primarily for their **scale** — each has built systems, products, and operating models that work reliably across enormous size and complexity. Beyond scale, I'm drawn to how deeply data and product thinking are embedded into their DNA, particularly the way they structure data science and product roles to directly influence product and business strategy. Studying how these companies operate — their product understanding, data science org structures, and decision-making — is directly relevant to my own goal: I see myself working at a company like Google as a **Senior Product Data Scientist / Lead** within the next 5–6 years, and understanding these companies closely is part of how I plan to get there.

## 4. Which business function do I currently understand the least?

My weakest areas are **Finance, Strategy, and Leadership**. These are less intuitive to me than analytics-adjacent functions like Marketing or Operations, since I haven't had direct exposure to how financial statements, capital allocation, competitive strategy, or leadership/people decisions actually get made in practice. Strengthening my understanding here is a key reason I'm pursuing this further education — these are the areas that separate a strong analyst from a well-rounded business leader.

## 5. If I became CEO of my current company tomorrow, what are the first three metrics I would want on my dashboard?

1. **GMV (Gross Merchandise Value)** — the clearest top-line signal of overall business scale and demand.
2. **P&L (Profit & Loss)** — to understand whether that scale is translating into a healthy, sustainable, profitable business.
3. **Ongoing Project Efficiency** — to track whether current initiatives and investments are actually delivering value relative to the resources being spent on them, so I can prioritize and cut ruthlessly where needed.

Together, these three give a fast read on **growth, profitability, and execution efficiency** — the three things I'd want visibility into from day one.
