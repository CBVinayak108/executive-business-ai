# Course Review — *The Product Management for AI & Data Science Course*

**Provider:** Udemy  
**Instructor:** Danielle Thé / 365 Careers  
**Format:** 12 sections, 68 lectures, about 4 h 54 m  
**Status:** In progress  
**Reviewed:** 2026-08-26

## Verdict

A useful foundation for the **AI/data product manager** perspective. It is most valuable for strengthening product framing, data/AI lifecycle fluency, stakeholder translation, and responsible deployment.

It is **not sufficient on its own** for the immediate goal of senior product analytics or product data science interviews. It should be paired with deeper work on product metrics, funnel analysis, experiment design and analysis, causal reasoning, SQL execution, and decision communication.

## What this course covers well

- PM role, product versus project management, and the analytics-translator role.
- AI/data strategy: deciding when AI is warranted, SWOT, hypotheses, and AI business canvas.
- User problem discovery, research, personas, prototyping, and prioritisation.
- Data sourcing, data quality, minimal viable data, and AI product development.
- Model evaluation, deployment approaches, monitoring, feedback loops, and error recovery.
- Cross-functional data/AI team workflow, ethics, privacy, and bias.

## Gaps to fill outside the course

1. **Product metric systems:** North-star metric, metric trees, input/guardrail metrics, cohorts, retention, monetisation, and marketplace measures.
2. **Funnel analytics:** event taxonomy, drop-off diagnosis, segmentation, qualitative follow-up, and prioritisation.
3. **Experimentation:** randomisation unit, sample-size/power concepts, primary metrics, guardrails, novelty effects, SRM, sequential testing, and interpreting uncertainty.
4. **Causal inference:** selection bias, confounding, quasi-experiments, difference-in-differences, and when an A/B test is infeasible.
5. **Execution:** PRDs, analytics plans, instrumentation, stakeholder cadence, launch criteria, and post-launch reviews.
6. **Technical practice:** SQL for product analysis, practical Python/R analysis, and clear executive narratives.

## How to use it

Do not take extensive passive notes. For each priority lesson, record one reusable artifact in this folder:

- the decision it improves;
- the framework in your own words;
- an applied example from e-commerce, retail SaaS, or fintech;
- one interview-ready story or answer.

The course should feed the practical sequence in the [learning track](README.md), rather than delay it.
